📚 Step-by-Step Recycling Guides

Practical recycling tips and step-by-step guides to keep materials out of landfill.
♻️ Know Your Bins ▼
Recycling (Blue) – clean and dry plastics #1–#2, glass jars, aluminum cans, paper, and cardboard.
Compost (Green) – food scraps, coffee grounds, tea bags, leaves, and small branches.
Trash (Black/Gray) – plastic bags, styrofoam, chip bags, diapers, and food-soiled paper.
Hazardous Waste – batteries, paint, motor oil, electronics, and fluorescent bulbs. Drop off at a certified center.
🧼 Prep Recyclables ▼
Rinse it. A quick rinse removes food and grease that can ruin an entire batch.
Remove caps/lids. Twist off plastic caps and recycle separately if accepted locally; put lids in trash if unsure.
Flatten cardboard. Breaking down boxes saves space in the bin and makes collection easier.
Keep it loose. Never bag recyclables loose in plastic bags — they will not be opened and will be landfilled.
🥡 Avoid Common Mistakes ▼
Plastic bags go in the trash or at grocery store drop-offs — never in the curbside bin.
Greasy pizza boxes and paper towels belong in compost or trash; oil-soaked paper is not recyclable.
Broken glass is a safety hazard. Wrap it tightly and place it in the trash, or check for a local glass recycling program.
Coffee pods and K-cups are usually trash unless your program explicitly accepts mixed materials.
🏠 Start at Home ▼
Set up two bins. Keep a recycling bin in the kitchen and a larger one in the garage.
Use a cheat sheet. Tape the local list of accepted and rejected items on your fridge.
Empty one at a time. Rinse and sort items right after use instead of letting them pile up.
Check locally. Rules vary by city. Visit your trash hauler or city website for Irvine-specific recycling updates and pickup schedules.
🏭 Specialty Recycling ▼
Electronics (e-waste). Drop off at a certified drop-off point or scheduled HHW event — never trash.
Batteries. Single-use, rechargeable, and button cells need separate collection to prevent fires.
Plastic bags & film. Return to grocery store drop-offs; curbside bins will reject them.
Textiles. Donate wearable clothing; use textile recycling bins for worn items — they become new fiber.
Appliances. Refrigerators and other large devices may require a special pick-up or eco-fee return program.
